# FLAGS

Every feature is built to completion and shipped **off**. This file is the
register: what each flag gates, and what must be true before it can be turned on.

The rule that keeps this safe: **a flag with no written precondition is a
bug waiting to be enabled.** Unreleased code rots — dependencies move, schema
changes, the framework upgrades. If a feature sits off for a year and someone
flips it, it will be broken unless the preconditions were written down and
re-checked.

Three kinds of gate:
- **ADMIN** — a pure runtime toggle. Admin dashboard can flip it, no deploy.
- **ADMIN + SETUP** — toggle exists, but external setup must be finished first.
- **CODE** — cannot be a runtime toggle. Needs a deploy, a build, or a policy
  that doesn't exist yet. Do not promise these to the admin dashboard.

## Who may flip one

**The platform admin, and nobody else** *(decided 2026-10-24)*. The register is
readable by every staff role — knowing that `commerce.checkout` is off is part of
being staff, and `setting_bool()` is how code asks. Writing is not: a switch is a
product decision about what the platform does, which is the owner's, and the
three other staff roles each have a job that does not include deciding it.

Until 2026-10-24 the write policy was `is_privileged()`, which meant a support
agent could turn checkout on with one UPDATE. No screen offered it, and that was
the whole of the protection — an offer is not a permission check. It is now
enforced by `platform_settings_authority_guard`, a trigger on the table, so the
refusal is the same whichever door the write comes through:
`only the platform admin may change a feature switch; commerce.checkout may be
read, not flipped`. The same trigger gives each control its own writer's gate, so
a control cannot be changed by writing the table and skipping its bounds, its
reason and its audit.

Measured, the register holds **108 rows: the 90 flags above plus these eighteen.**
Ninety-two are boolean switches, thirteen are integers and three are text (a
currency, a room's base URL, and the safeguarding lead). The two
governance policy controls have no gate because they are not features; the money
controls are ADMIN, because a number that changes what a total says is changed
from the dashboard and not from a migration — and so are the upload quota, the
board control and the refund window, for the same reason: a number that decides
what a teacher may store, a switch that lets a board decide instead of the owner,
or the length of the window in which a family can ask for their money back, is a
decision and not a deploy.

## What this register shows

**1. The admin dashboard was the critical path, and it is built.**
`platform.admin_settings` had to exist before any other flag meant anything, and
it was missing from the plan: no settings table, no endpoints. `platform_settings`
now holds the register, `/admin` reads and toggles it, and `/api/admin/flags`
writes it. A trigger refuses to enable a `code`-gated flag at runtime, so the
dashboard cannot promise what needs a deploy.

**2. CODE-gated switches cannot be turned on at runtime.**
At the earlier register snapshot, `gate_type='code'` was on 11 flags. Each named something that needed a deploy
rather than a click: a provider, a written policy, a legal decision, or a design
that was never done. At that snapshot they were `learning.ai_support`, `learning.recitation`,
`platform.incident`, `platform.takedown`, `platform.data_export`,
`platform.withdrawal_right`, `platform.accessibility`, `family.child_login`,
`teaching.payouts`, `delivery.transcode` — and `platform.incident` is CODE for a
method rather than a missing screen, since detection counts and never judges.
`platform.withdrawal_right` left the "not built" list on 2026-11-01 and is the one
flag in this register that ships **on**, because the owner decided what the
checkout should ask and the migration that built it is the deploy that turns a
CODE flag on. It is safe to leave on: with no refunds policy in force the question
is not asked and nobody's 14 days are touched.

The table above and current `platform_settings` are authoritative for today's gates;
`family.child_login` became ADMIN + SETUP again in migration 650.
The list is re-counted from `platform_settings` rather than from prose, because
five units left it stale in one day and a stale total outlives the reason it was
written. On 2026-10-26 `commerce.referrals`, `commerce.sponsorship`,
`teaching.applications`, `learning.multi_language` and all four `institution.*`
flags became ADMIN. On 2026-10-28 `teaching.content_licence` followed, because the
gate was written and the decision to refuse an upload belongs to the owner; then
`commerce.institutions` followed, because a school buying seats turned out to need
no payment provider at all — an invoice, a code and a claim. Some of those moves
are worth reading twice. `institution.sso` moved although SAML and OIDC are still
missing, because the part that can exist — a verified email domain — is built;
`learning.multi_language` moved although the interface is still English, because a
course saying which language it is taught in is the half that needs no translator.
On 2026-10-30 two more left this list on the owner's six answers:
`platform.age_assurance`, because a declaration that says it is a declaration needs
no provider, `platform.safeguarding`, because the check, the acknowledgement
and the named lead now exist and the gate is enforced, and `learning.ijazah`,
because the owner decided a register of who authorised whom is the owner's own to
keep and needs no panel to exist first. `teaching.screening` did not
move — it was **removed**, on the same day, because it was a second name for
exactly the decision `platform.safeguarding` enforces and its precondition (*no
acknowledgement, no refusal*) had stopped being true. One decision has one row; a
register that carries two is what the owner reads by mistake.
A switch nobody can turn on while the feature it names works is a worse lie than a
screen that says what is absent.

**On 2026-11-02 seven more were removed rather than reworded**, and the reason is
worth keeping because it is the failure this document exists to prevent. Seven
rows carried no written precondition at all — the em-dash placeholder — and
`flags.test.mjs` holds this table and `platform_settings` to each other, so the two
agreed with one another and neither had ever been compared with the software. One
at a time, against the tree: `family.profiles` shipped and is always on, because a
guardian reserving a login for a child is a custody question and not a feature gate;
`teaching.dashboard` *is* `/teach`, and a teacher without their own course list has
no product; `teaching.invites` was the second name for `teaching.invite_links`, the
first duplicate found after screening; `commerce.free` had nothing to switch, since
a free course is a price of zero and an enrolment source that already exists;
`platform.audit` had no table, no writer and no screen behind it; and
`platform.maintenance_mode` was never built, and could not be a toggle, because
every write goes through PostgREST and a real read-only mode has to be enforced in
the database — the design is in `docs/remaining.md` so the want is not lost.
`platform.terms_versioning` went too: its precondition said *built 2026-10-27*,
which was true, but nothing reads the key, so it was a row that told the owner a
feature was ready and did nothing when flipped.

That gives the register a rule, and `supabase/tests/register_truth.test.mjs` now
enforces it: **a flag may be unread only while its precondition says what is
missing. A row that claims it is ready — *None…*, the word this document uses —
must be read by code.** A switch that claims readiness and changes nothing is worse
than a missing one, because the owner flips it and believes the platform is broken.

A further **17 are ADMIN + SETUP**: the toggle is real, but a provider, a sender
domain or a written policy has to be finished first. Those are the ones where
turning it on too early does damage.

**3. Six flags are blocked on facts you don't have, not on code.**
`commerce.checkout`, `mobile_money`, `subscriptions`, `refunds`,
`teaching.panel`, `family.guardian_verification`. No amount of building finishes
these. Four of the six now have their code: refunds are decided at
`/admin/refunds`, guardian verification runs at `/family/guardians` and
`/admin/guardians`, checkout creates orders and subscriptions allocate seats.
What is missing is a provider, a jurisdiction, or a policy decision — and
`teaching.panel` is still an open question, not a backlog item.

**4. Nothing in this register is still blocked on design that was never done.**
`family.engagement` was the last one, and the owner designed it on 2026-10-30: a
private streak and a shelf of badges for the child and their family, with no
points, no leaderboards and nothing compared across children. That was the whole
of the "mechanics undesigned" objection, so it is built rather than merely
permitted. `learning.discussion` left this category earlier on the same terms —
moderation settled, authority posts visible, learner posts held — and so did
`learning.reviews`. What remains unbuilt in this register is now blocked on a
**fact** (a provider, a policy, a jurisdiction) or on the owner's own decision, and
neither of those is a design that can be finished by writing code.

## Suggested build order, given build-everything

Not phasing — sequencing so nothing is built on sand.

1. `platform.admin_settings` + the flag layer itself. Nothing else is
   switchable without it.
2. Identity, family, enrolment, lessons, progress, discussion.
   Everything here is unblocked and fully specified.
3. Teaching invites, authoring, dashboard, peer review.
   Unblocked, and it's what your teacher friends are waiting for.
4. Commerce interfaces with **no implementation** — a `PaymentProvider`
   boundary and empty adapters. Build the shape, not the guess.
5. Everything blocked on a fact: build the boundary, leave the adapter empty,
   and write the precondition here so future-you knows why it's empty.
